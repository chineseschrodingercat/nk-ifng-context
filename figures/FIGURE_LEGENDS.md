Whiskers propagate image-reading bounds only, not biological uncertainty. C, Complete IFNγ-by-KIR-blockade arms from Wang et al. Fig. 2B, using primary NK cells and THP-1 targets at E:T 8:1 [1]. D, IFNγ-by-ICAM-1-overexpression arms from the same source Fig. 4E, with THP-1 and NK-92MI. Lines in C and D guide the eye across categorical interventions, not continuous exposure or paired biological units. The source displays representative technical-triplicate means; the other displayed cytokine arms are retained in the data tables. Reconstructed interactions quantify published experiments and do not claim a new molecular mechanism.

Figure 2. Death-ligand blockade sensitivity can diverge from net cytotoxicity. A, Four neuroblastoma models from Sheard et al., at the displayed 10 ng/ml IFNγ condition with anti-TRAIL [15]. Clearance is 100 minus no-NK-normalized eight-hour calcein survival. B, HT-29 with four NK activation states and two E:T ratios, measured by four-hour chromium-release lysis in Mori et al. [16]. Circles are the net IFNγ effect without blockade; squares are the unblocked-minus-blocked IFNγ effects. Horizontal whiskers propagate image-reading bounds, not biological confidence intervals. The zero line is no change on the plotted scale. Both sources display representative experiments; shared controls, doses and assay contexts were not counted as independent replications. Complete source arms, other doses and log-survival sensitivity remain in the accompanying CSVs. Positive interaction values identify an operational blockade-sensitive component, not an exclusive target death route.

B, IFNγ secretion with soluble antibody (8 paired donors). C, IFNγ secretion after immobilized-antibody stimulation (8 paired donors). Lines connect source-paired observations within each panel; donor identities are not linked across panels. D, Anti-TRAIL-minus-isotype difference in remaining targets, with 12 donor means grouped into four shared-reference batches. Colors distinguish batches; black diamonds denote batch means. Units, all seven endpoints, t intervals and multiplicity-adjusted tests are in Table 3. These experiments do not manipulate exogenous IFNγ and are not additional IFNγ-response validation cohorts.

Figure 4. Ligand measurements do not establish a stable prediction rule in the pediatric panel. A, Model response summaries and resolved donor markers from Aquino-López et al. [2]. The same four NK donors were shared across 22 models; donor values summarize six source E:T settings. Vertical offsets distinguish coincident symbols. Diamonds mark complete four-donor means; open squares and reading intervals show the two interval-constrained model means. B, Family-weighted leave-family-out RMSE for fixed ridge models at penalty 10, using 22 models in 21 families; CHLA-9 and CHLA-10 were held out together. The dashed reference is the training-fold mean. Recognition denotes ICAM1/MHC-I/HLA-E and death denotes FAS/DR4/DR5. All transforms were fitted within training folds. These are within-study model-family predictions, not independent-study validation. Table 2 and the data package preserve matched-subset and molecular sensitivities.

Figure 5. An additional target-cell panel illustrates the limits of context transfer. A, All 17 target models in Wang et al. Fig. 6 [1]. Blue circles use the common 1:1 E:T point for 16 models; the asterisk identifies SUP-B15 at 2:1. Orange squares retain each model’s higher ratio, shown in its row label. Blue whiskers are deterministic reading bounds; higher-ratio bounds remain in the data table. The source reports technical-triplicate mean ± SD from a representative experiment of at least two; no biological response classification is inferred from the reading bounds. The 293T model is transformed rather than a conventional malignant tumor line. B, Kasumi-1 is the sole exact model overlap with the pediatric panel [2]. Wang values are individual E:T summaries, whereas Aquino is the mean of four donors after averaging six E:T settings per donor. These are differently defined study summaries with an opposite response direction, not a controlled comparison of effector type or an estimated between-study causal effect. No line connects the studies.

Figure 6. Recognition and death-associated selection patterns vary across public genetic-screen contexts. The fixed 21-gene panel across all 11 Dufva Table S4 result screens [19]. Brackets mark the seven loss-of-function screens on the left and four gain-of-function screens on the right; the vertical divider separates these groups. Each tile is 2 × the within-screen percentile of its published gene effect − 1. Red denotes a higher relative selection rank and blue a lower rank; zero denotes the screen median, not a null effect. KHYG1 is marked explicitly; the remaining columns use primary NK cells under source-specific conditions. No significance is encoded by color. All 222,609 result rows, original selected effect fields, FDR values and the 18-entry design/93-sample metadata are retained. The display does not equate gene selection with acute lysis, infer IFNγ-addition effects, or treat genes as independent biological replications.
